<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When most people think about mindfulness, they picture someone sitting cross-legged on a cushion in a quiet room, surrounded by candles and soft music. While there&#8217;s certainly value in meditation, I&#8217;ve found that some of the most effective mindfulness practices happen during ordinary moments throughout the day.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mindfulness isn&#8217;t about escaping life; it&#8217;s about becoming more present in it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Recently, my watch has been giving me problems. Every time it tries to update, it gets stuck on the restart screen and forces me to manually restart it. It&#8217;s a small inconvenience, but at first it drove me crazy. I like efficiency. I like things to work properly. Like many people, I often find myself moving from one task to the next, trying to get as much done as possible. Having to stop and wait on a watch felt like one more thing getting in the way.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then, I started paying attention to when it was happening.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">More often than not, the watch freezes when I&#8217;ve been sitting at my desk for too long. I&#8217;ve been answering emails, working on Operation Antifragile projects, building programs, or trying to solve ten different problems at once. My mind starts racing, my patience gets shorter, and I&#8217;m often no longer doing my best work. I&#8217;m just spinning my wheels.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When the watch freezes, my first reaction is usually frustration.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;I don&#8217;t have time for this.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;I have too much to do.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;This stupid watch won&#8217;t work.&#8221;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sound familiar?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The truth is that those are often the exact moments when I need to slow down the most.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead of getting frustrated, I&#8217;ve started treating it as a reminder (at least, I try to). Take a breath. Step back for a moment. Slow down. Most of the time, thirty seconds of intentional breathing does more for my productivity and mental state than another thirty minutes of trying to force my way through a problem.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The finger I use to restart the watch adds another layer to the practice. It&#8217;s the same finger affected by my C5-C6 nerve injury. Years later, the area is still numb, and I&#8217;ve spent countless hours trying to rebuild the connection between my brain and the muscles affected by that injury.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So while I&#8217;m holding the button, I intentionally focus on that connection. I pay attention to the pressure of my finger against the button. I focus on the signal traveling from my brain to my hand. I try to create that mind-to-muscle connection and wake those nerves up.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Maybe it helps. Maybe it doesn&#8217;t.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But I know that recovery requires attention, repetition, and intention. If there&#8217;s even a chance that those few seconds of focused awareness help reinforce that connection, then it&#8217;s time well spent.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">At the very least, it reminds me that progress is still possible and that some things simply can&#8217;t be rushed.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That&#8217;s really what mindfulness is.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It&#8217;s not about emptying your mind, sitting perfectly still for an hour, or pretending stress doesn&#8217;t exist.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mindfulness is simply paying attention.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It&#8217;s recognizing when you&#8217;re rushing, reacting emotionally, or operating on autopilot, and then making the conscious decision to return to the present moment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The problem is that most of us spend very little time there. We&#8217;re replaying conversations from yesterday, worrying about tomorrow, checking our phones, thinking about our next task, or stressing about things we can&#8217;t control. We become so focused on what&#8217;s next that we stop experiencing what&#8217;s right in front of us.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mindfulness brings us back.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It can happen during a workout when you focus on your breathing between sets. It can happen while paddling on the water and paying attention to the rhythm of your stroke. It can happen during a walk, while drinking your morning coffee, or while sitting quietly for a few moments before starting your day.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It can even happen while waiting for a watch to restart.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The more frequently we practice these small moments of awareness, the easier it becomes to stay grounded when life gets chaotic. We become more aware of our stress response. We make better decisions. We regulate our emotions more effectively. We stop reacting to everything and start responding with intention.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Those small moments add up.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Over time, they can improve focus, reduce stress, enhance recovery, and help us maintain a healthier relationship with ourselves and the people around us.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ironically, it was a malfunctioning watch that reminded me of that lesson.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">What started as an annoyance became a cue.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So, the next time life presents a small inconvenience, consider viewing it differently. Instead of seeing it as an obstacle, ask yourself if it could become a reminder.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because sometimes the obstacle isn&#8217;t getting in the way, rather it&#8217;s showing you exactly what you need.</p>
