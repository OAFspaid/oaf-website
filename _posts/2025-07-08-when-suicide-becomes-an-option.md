<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A recent poll in a veteran group raised the question:<br>Are some people biologically wired for suicidal ideation—or is it the result of trauma and life experience?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s probably a combination of both.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But we need to talk about something else that’s contributing to the problem—and no one wants to say it out loud:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Normalization through repetition.</strong></p>
</blockquote>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sometimes these thoughts don’t show up because we’re struggling.<br>They show up because they’ve been <em>suggested</em> so often that they start to feel like part of the script.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every time you call the VA…<br><strong>“Are you suicidal today?”</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That question gets repeated so frequently that it stops feeling like concern—and starts feeling like conditioning.<br><strong>Is this constant repetition actually driving suicide rates up?</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s a question we <em>need</em> to be asking.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A Personal Story</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">After I stepped away from the fire service, I started hearing rumors:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>“We thought you were suicidal.”</em></p>
</blockquote>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Why?<br>Because I have PTSD.<br>Because I talk about Operator Syndrome.<br>Because I’m honest about the struggles that come with this life.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But I wasn’t suicidal.<br>I didn’t want to die.<br>I’ve always been an advocate—for myself and for others.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Still, something changed.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Intrusive thoughts started creeping in.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Not because I was in danger…<br>But because the <em>system</em> wouldn’t stop planting the idea in my head.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>“Are you thinking about harming yourself?”</em><br>That’s asked <strong>every single time</strong> I talk to the VA.</p>
</blockquote>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And when I was part of a program where one of my answers slightly differed from the norm, I got a call the next day.<br>Not a check-in.<br>An interrogation.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Someone I’d never met was on the phone grilling me about whether I was suicidal.<br>No context. No connection. Just protocol.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s not support.<br>That’s surveillance.<br>That’s how we lose trust.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And the more it happens, the more those thoughts start to feel <em>normal</em>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That’s not care.<br>That’s <em>programming.</em><br>And it’s time we break that cycle.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Stats Are Real</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>An estimated <strong>17–22 veterans die by suicide every day</strong></li>



<li>First responder suicide rates are <strong>54% higher</strong> than the general population</li>



<li>Nearly <strong>47% of firefighters</strong> have reported suicidal thoughts</li>



<li>Most never ask for help until it’s too late</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These numbers don’t lie. But the way we’re addressing them <em>might</em> be making it worse.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What Needs to Change</h2>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Stop leading with suicide assessments.</strong> Lead with <strong>human connection.</strong></li>



<li>Create real <strong>space to say “I’m not okay”</strong> without assuming we’re at risk.</li>



<li>We need <strong>movement, breath, and purpose</strong>—not more diagnostic labels.</li>



<li>And we need to remember: You are not your diagnosis.<br>You are not your trauma.<br>You are not broken.</li>
</ul>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
