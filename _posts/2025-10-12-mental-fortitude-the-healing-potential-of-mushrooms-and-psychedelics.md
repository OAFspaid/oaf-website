<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong><em>Why it’s time to explore natural medicine for nerve regeneration, trauma, and brain health</em></strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">By: Matt Spaid</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Strength doesn&#8217;t just come from your muscles. Every lift, every contraction, every ounce of performance is a conversation between the brain and body. The nervous system significantly influences both the amount of weight you can lift and your overall movement capabilities. When that connection breaks, as it did for me after a cervical-spine injury, you realize how fragile that system truly is.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A few years ago, I suffered a <strong>C5–C6 nerve injury</strong> that shut down my right pec, tricep, and lat. Years of carrying heavy loads as a Marine grunt led to &#8220;military neck,&#8221; a condition where my C-spine lost its natural curve. Additionally, prolonged helmet wear as a Firefighter caused a right head tilt, which my brain began to perceive as straight. This led to a lot of damage over the years, and one night when I was sleeping at the station, I suddenly experienced intense pain and muscle spasms. Eventually, my arm became numb and immobile. My pec and tricep withered away in a short amount of time. It’s a hard thing to explain. You look at your arm, tell it to move, and nothing happens. No contraction. No signal. Just silence.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When I saw the neurosurgeon, he told me it wouldn&#8217;t matter how many curls I did; I wouldn&#8217;t grow the muscle back. He wasn&#8217;t sure if I would be able to continue firefighting. I was facing surgery, but they were also worried I would come out of the surgery worse due to the significant damage and deterioration of my neck. They gave me a month to see if I could recover. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I started small, as I always do, and accomplished what I could. I used a variety of methods, mainly incorporating blood flow restriciton with isometircs. I would also perform tricep extensions with a 5 pound indian club. At first, I could barely get three shaky reps. Then one day, I tried something new: <strong>a psilocybin microdose.</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That day, I went from three reps to thirty-four. The muscle woke up. It was as if the signal that had been blocked suddenly broke through. I was able to grow about 1.5&#8243; back on my arm in a month, avoided surgery, and went back to firefighting.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This begs us to ask the question, why are we not doing more research on the medicinal applications of mushrooms? I combined microdosing with breathwork, red-light therapy, mobility, and strength training. Over time, my strength returned, and my nervous system not only healed but also learned to reconnect. I also noticed a significant improvement in my mental health.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>A Word of Caution: The Need for Foundation and Responsibility</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s vital to have a strong foundation <strong>spiritually, mentally, and physically</strong> before using any psychedelic or entheogenic therapy. These substances can open doors, but they also open wounds. Without grounding, guidance, and integration, they can lead people down dark paths.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Psychedelics are not a one-size-fits-all solution.</strong> They are powerful tools that must be used <strong>responsibly</strong>, in <strong>safe, formal, and supportive settings</strong> with proper guidance.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If approached with respect, intention, and structure, they can help people heal and grow. But without those foundations, they can amplify instability instead of resolving it. I believe it should be viewed as medicine. However, medicine can certainly be abused.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is why education, mentorship, and preparation are critical.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Nature’s Pharmacy: Functional Mushrooms and Nerve Regeneration</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Beyond &#8220;magic mushrooms,&#8221; which represent a small fraction of healing fungi, <strong>functional mushrooms</strong> such as <strong>lion&#8217;s mane, cordyceps, and turkey tail</strong> provide a vast array of healing compounds. These compounds support the brain and nervous system without inducing a psychedelic experience.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Lion’s Mane – The Neural Architect</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Lion’s mane has shown the most promise for nerve regeneration. Compounds called <em>hericenones</em> and <em>erinacines</em> stimulate <strong>nerve-growth factor (NGF)</strong>, the molecule responsible for repairing and creating new neural pathways (Ly et al., 2018).</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Animal studies show that lion’s mane can accelerate <strong>peripheral-nerve recovery</strong> after injury, while human studies reveal improved cognitive performance and reduced inflammation. I now take <strong>5–10 grams daily</strong> to support nerve health and brain recovery and I’ve felt the difference.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Cordyceps – Oxygen, Energy, and Neuroprotection</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cordyceps supports mitochondrial function, improving oxygen utilization and cellular energy output. Research shows <strong>anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective</strong> effects in models of memory loss and neurodegenerative disease (Liu-Ambrose et al., 2010; PMC5874985).</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Turkey Tail – The Immune–Brain Connection</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Turkey tail may not directly regrow nerves, but its <strong>immune-modulating</strong> and <strong>gut-brain</strong> effects create the right internal environment for healing. The gut–brain axis influences everything from mood to inflammation to recovery capacity.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Together, these mushrooms create a synergy that supports both <strong>neural repair</strong> and <strong>mental resilience.</strong></p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Psychedelics and Mental Health: Rediscovering What We Lost</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Psychedelics are not new. For decades, they were legally and effectively used to treat depression, addiction, and trauma. In the 1950s and 60s, psychiatrists treated thousands of patients with LSD and psilocybin for deep psychological wounds.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then the research stopped. Not because science failed, but because politics did.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Now, nearly seventy years later, those same therapies are making a comeback. Major studies from <strong>Johns Hopkins, MAPS, and Imperial College London</strong> show psilocybin and MDMA-assisted therapy producing <strong>major breakthroughs</strong> in PTSD, depression, and anxiety.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And the results aren’t just academic. Veterans are traveling overseas to Mexico, Costa Rica, and Peru for psychedelic-assisted therapy. They come back changed. They come back <em>alive.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These treatments work. But veterans shouldn’t have to cross borders or spend thousands of dollars for care we could responsibly provide here at home.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Veterans deserve access to <strong>safe, guided, and evidence-based psychedelic therapy</strong> in the United States. They’ve carried the weight for us; it’s time we carry it for them.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Science Validates What the Body Remembers</strong></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">What I felt when that muscle “woke up” tracks with what labs are discovering about how psychedelics and mushrooms reshape the brain.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Psilocybin &amp; structural plasticity:</strong> In mice, a single psilocybin dose increased dendritic-spine size and density by ~10 % within 24 hours, persisting for a month (PubMed 34228959; PMC8376772).<br></li>



<li><strong>Synaptic strength:</strong> Psilocybin enhances synaptic signaling via BDNF and mTOR pathways (Front Mol Neurosci 2021).<br></li>



<li><strong>Lion’s mane &amp; NGF:</strong><strong><br></strong>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Double-blind RCTs in older adults with mild cognitive impairment showed <strong>improved cognitive scores</strong> over 16 weeks (HDS-R tests).<br></li>



<li>Erinacines and hericenones stimulate <strong>NGF synthesis</strong> and <strong>neurite outgrowth</strong> in neuronal cultures (PMC5987239).<br></li>



<li>New data show extracts enlarge <strong>neuronal growth-cone size</strong>, supporting connection formation (UQ News 2023).<br></li>



<li>A pilot human trial in healthy adults found faster reaction times and reduced stress after 28 days (PMC10675414).<br></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">These findings don’t prove miracles, but they explain the mechanistic “why” behind lived stories like mine. Neuroplasticity, growth-factor signaling, and synaptic resilience are real and measurable.</p>
