<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><em>Stop Chasing Symptoms. Start Healing the Root.</em></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Article By: Matt Spaid</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There’s a powerful nerve running through your body that influences almost every major system—yet it’s often overlooked in traditional medicine and even functional health circles. It’s called the <strong>vagus nerve</strong>, and if it isn’t functioning properly, <em>nothing else</em> may fully heal.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I’ve been diving into the book <strong>&#8220;Accessing the Healing Power of the Vagus Nerve&#8221; by Stanley Rosenberg</strong>, and it&#8217;s opening up a whole new framework for understanding healing—especially for veterans, first responders, and tactical professionals dealing with chronic pain, PTSD, or what many of us know as Operator Syndrome.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Is the Vagus Nerve?</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The vagus nerve is the <strong>tenth cranial nerve</strong> and the main communication highway of the <strong>parasympathetic nervous system</strong>—the &#8220;rest and digest&#8221; system. It’s the longest cranial nerve in the body, running from the brainstem down through the face, neck, heart, lungs, and into the digestive organs.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It has <strong>two primary branches</strong>:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>The <strong>ventral vagus</strong> (linked to social engagement, calm, and healthy organ function)<br></li>



<li>The <strong>dorsal vagus</strong> (associated with freeze response, dissociation, and visceral shutdown)<br></li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When this system is out of balance, it doesn&#8217;t just affect your <em>stress</em>—it can impact your breathing, digestion, heart rate, immune function, mental clarity, and more.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Case in Point: COPD and the Vagus Nerve</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One example Rosenberg gives is <strong>COPD</strong>. Most people think COPD is just a lung problem, but what if it’s also a <em>nerve regulation</em> problem?</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>The <strong>dorsal vagus</strong> constricts the bronchioles, reducing airflow.<br></li>



<li>The <strong>sympathetic system</strong> opens them up for maximum oxygen intake (fight or flight).<br></li>



<li>But in a healthy state, the <strong>ventral vagus</strong> should relax the bronchioles—allowing smooth, regulated airflow in and out of the lungs.<br></li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If the ventral branch isn’t working properly, <em>no amount of medication will fix the root issue</em>. You’re stuck chasing symptoms like the Hydra from Greek mythology—cutting off one head just to have another grow back.</p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Autonomic Dysfunction = Systemic Dysfunction</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many conditions—chronic fatigue, digestive problems, anxiety, depression, trauma response, even autoimmune issues—may stem from <strong>autonomic nervous system imbalance</strong>, especially <em>vagus nerve dysfunction</em>.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Think about it:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>If you’re constantly in sympathetic overdrive (fight or flight), your <strong>digestive enzymes shut down</strong>, immune function tanks, and breathing patterns become dysfunctional.<br></li>



<li>If the <strong>dorsal vagus</strong> dominates, you may experience numbness, disconnection, poor circulation, low energy, and shutdown.<br></li>



<li>If the <strong>ventral vagus</strong> is strong, you feel safe, grounded, clear-minded, and resilient.<br></li>
</ul>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>A Simple Test for Vagal Function</strong></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here’s one test from Rosenberg’s book:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>Have the person sit comfortably.<br></li>



<li>Ask them to open their mouth and say: <strong>&#8220;Ah, ah, ah, ah&#8221;</strong> in short, percussive bursts.<br></li>



<li>Observe the soft palate and arches on either side of the uvula.<br></li>



<li>If both arches rise symmetrically, <strong>vagal tone is likely strong</strong>.<br></li>



<li>If one side doesn’t lift, there may be <strong>a dysfunction in the ventral branch</strong> of the vagus.<br></li>
</ol>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is just one simple diagnostic tool—yet it reveals a much deeper truth: <em>if the communication system of the body is broken, healing becomes an uphill battle.</em></p>



<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why This Matters for Veterans and First Responders</strong></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many of us in the tactical world have lived in fight-or-flight for years—or decades. The cost?</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Chronic gut issues<br></li>



<li>Breathing disorders<br></li>



<li>Numbness or emotional disconnection<br></li>



<li>Chronic fatigue<br></li>



<li>Difficulty healing from injury<br></li>



<li>Difficulty finding peace<br></li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is <em>Operator Syndrome</em> through the lens of <strong>polyvagal theory</strong>. Your nervous system may not be broken—it may just need to be retrained.</p>
